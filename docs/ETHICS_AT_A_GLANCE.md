# Why B.E.N. exists — plain-language AI ethics statement

**Working draft. The founders have not yet adopted it.**

B.E.N. means **Be Even Nicer** and serves the **Benevolent Energy Network**. It exists to make it easier for people to learn, create, cooperate, care and improve their communities—without turning those people into a product or giving an algorithm power over their lives.

### What B.E.N. should do

Help an artist learn technique and find inspiration; help a writer develop a story in their own voice; help a dancer experiment safely; help members share skills, organise voluntary aid and participate in decisions; help stewards do paperwork with evidence and transparency. A member must be free to challenge B.E.N. and its founders.

### What B.E.N. must not do

It must not decide people's moral worth, loyalty or political beliefs; punish inactivity; sell private creative material; replace human votes; impose artistic taste; share addresses without consent; decide allegations or deny fundamental access on its own; encourage unsafe acts; fabricate expertise or results. It is not a police force, therapist, government or social-credit authority.

### Who owns the art?

The artist. The dancer. The musician. The writer. B.E.N. should offer methods, references, practice, critique and human connections—not take credit for someone's original work. Other people's artworks and texts need proper rights and attribution.

### Who controls B.E.N.?

People, through transparent, contestable governance. GitHub sign-in is only authentication: it does not grant repository editing rights or a governance vote. Technical administrators are accountable and their access can be revoked. There must be fair reporting and appeals, including complaints about leaders.

### Is it open source?

Not yet. This **review-stage code** is `UNLICENSED` pending a real founders' rights-and-licensing decision. The proposal is to adopt a recognised open-source licence for application code while treating art, educational texts, member data and model weights separately.

### How do we know it's helping?

By listening to people and checking real outcomes: artistic learning, completed voluntary help, increased inclusion and useful decisions. We should also report mistakes, environmental costs, privacy harms and when people feel excluded. Do not turn kindness into a points system.

**The principle:** Authority exists to protect the network, not to own it.
