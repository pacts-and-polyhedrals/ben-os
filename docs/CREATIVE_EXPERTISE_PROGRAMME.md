# B.E.N. — Human Creativity Knowledge Programme

**48 creative disciplines · 48 editable teaching workpackets · 57 draft evaluation prompts**

This directory contains **structured original teaching scaffolds**, not a newly trained expert model, and none of the craft descriptions has undergone independent practitioner verification. The first build uses knowledge in prompts and a local model. Real expertise requires qualified human reviewers, source-backed lessons, continuous evaluation and permissioned contributions.

**Product philosophy:** each session should leave the human with new understanding, decisions, experience, or connection to another human creator. AI creative output is not itself the success measure.

## Recommended knowledge architecture

**Stage A — Foundations (implemented as drafts).** Every discipline gets techniques, a starter exercise, advanced challenge, reflective questions and a private practice journal. Tag beginner/intermediate/advanced competency. Include citations to original works, artists, museums or teaching bodies where permissions allow.

**Stage B — Practitioner review (not implemented).** Recruit practicing teachers or professionals for each family of disciplines. They approve factual technique guides, safety notes, translations and culturally diverse reference examples. Provide a rights/attribution ledger for every reference. Avoid treating one curator's taste as universal.

**Stage C — Retrieval and grounded coaching (not implemented).** Index only rights-cleared approved lesson snippets, with id, title, creator, licence, publication date, disciplinary tags and review status. Use retriever filters that check member age/access and rights. AI responses should point to source, quote only within permission and admit missing expertise.

**Stage D — Verified mentorship at scale (not implemented).** Implement opt-in mentor introductions, individual learning plans, event-led workshops, peer circles, apprenticeships and cultural exchange. Human teachers set boundaries and availability, members can block contacts. No public usefulness score, no mandatory contribution or monetisation of someone's private work.

**Stage E — Measure actual learning (not implemented).** Use voluntary before/after participant feedback, retained creative agency, skill-specific confidence, fairness, access and human collaboration; never rank people's worth or use progress as an eligibility gate.

## Quality review and evaluation standards

Each scenario should be scored by qualified humans on: technical correctness, creator autonomy, constructive critique, safety/accessibility, cultural range, citation/provenance and uncertainty. Separate beginner/intermediate/professional tasks. Add adversarial cases for copyright laundering, fabricated observations ('I saw your painting'), unwanted imitation, unsafe bodily exercises, private contact leaks and alleged organisational authority.

**No fabricated model benchmarks:** the repository contains 57 *questions* but no validated model responses or expert scores. `npm run eval:creative` previews them. Use `npm run eval:creative:run` against an installed model, then arrange independent human scoring before any competence claim.

## Example conversations

**Painting:** “I want to paint a rainy street.” B.E.N. clarifies intended mood, shows credited public-domain examples, explains value grouping and reflected light, suggests a three-value thumbnail and helps compare *the artist’s* thumbnails. It does not generate the final painting.

**Creative writing:** “My ending feels flat.” B.E.N. asks about character desire and change, explains tension/payoff, highlights where consequences could become clearer and suggests a revision experiment. It does not overwrite the author's distinctive voice or publish a story in their name.

**Dance:** “I have an eight-count phrase but need a transition.” B.E.N. explores rhythm, direction, weight and safe movement alternatives, offers ideas for rehearsal structure and encourages recording observations with consent. It does not claim to have observed the dancers unless footage was actually provided and processed.

**Music:** “My chorus isn't landing.” B.E.N. discusses phrasing, harmonic tension, texture and call-response, sets an instrument-based practice exercise and offers a human songwriting peer. It does not claim rights to the song.

**Community art:** “We want to paint a mural together.” B.E.N. helps plan accessibility, community consultation, legal permissions, collaborative decisions, material costs and consent before anyone starts.

## Creative rights rule

Human creators retain their work; member submissions are not used to train models by default. The repository's original curricula and code have **different** proposed licences; founders must approve terms and verify contributor rights before release. Any reference image used in the UI is accompanied by provenance; artists and rights-holders may request corrections/removal through the future contact route.
